
### **Common Signs of an Undercover Police Car in NSW:**

1. **Vehicle Makes & Models**
    
    - Common choices:
	    - Holden Commodores (especially older, clean models),
	    - Ford Falcons, 
	    - Toyota Camrys, 
	    - Hyundai iLoad vans, 
	    - Kia Sorentos, 
	    - and sometimes SUVs like Ford Everests.
    - They’re often late-model, unassuming, but well-maintained.
        
2. **Subtle Modifications & Features**
    - **Extra Antennas**: Look for multiple, short black antennas (for police radio, GPS, data).
    - **Dark, Non-Stock Wheels**: Plain hubcaps or steel rims, sometimes with heavy-duty tires.
    - **Unmarked Emergency Equipment**: Hidden LED lights in the grille, rear window, or mirror housings.
    - **Spotlights & Extra Mirrors**: Some have small, discreet spotlights near the side mirrors.
        
3. **Occupant Behavior**
    - Two or more similarly dressed occupants (often athletic, short haircuts, wearing casual but tidy clothing like polo shirts or jackets).
    - They may sit parked for long periods, avoid eye contact, or use laptops/tablets inside the vehicle.
        
4. **Registration & Plates**
    - Check for standard NSW plates (not “POLICE” marked), but sometimes they use standard-issue government plates or regular personalized plates.
    - You can look up registration details via Service NSW app—government vehicles may not show owner details.
        
5. **Interior Clues**
    - Look for police radios, computer mounts, or firearm safes between front seats.
    - No personal items (like kids’ seats, stickers, or clutter).

### **Advanced Tactics:**

- **Use a Radar Detector or Scanner**: Some pick up police frequencies or radar guns.
- **Observe Patrol Patterns**: Undercover cars often linger near known hotspots (nightlife areas, highways, transport hubs).
- **Dashcam Awareness**: Review footage—many undercover cars have distinctive features visible on video.

---

### ⚠️ **Final Warning:**

While these tips improve detection, undercover vehicles are designed to blend in. Always trust your instincts—if something feels off, it probably is.
