- Jean-Baptiste Lamarck posited the theory that evolution occurred in organisms based on their environment to fit their needs.
	- Giraffes for instance were thought to have necks elongated based on their environment.
	- It supposed that an individual organism could acquire new morphological characteristics **during it's lifetime** based on its needs. And when it reproduces the trait is passed on.
- Aristotle (384-322 BC) created a system for classifying living things (Scala Naturae aka the great chain of being).

> The Great Chain of Being (as thought by Aristotle)

- The top is God, then the angels, then humans (the highest form of living things), then the animals, then the plants.
- Aristotle believed that species were fixed entities, they didn't change.
	- This idea was challenged in the late 18th century when old fossils were discovered.
- Early reconstruction of fossils was quite difficult. 
	- Georges Cuvier (French anatomist) was one of the first to be able to posit reconstruction effectively. He realized that some species become extinct, which was entirely new and different from the Great Chain of Being.
	- He also noticed patterns in where fossils of distinct species were discovered; noticing that different types of strata contained different types of fossils with shallower strata containing species that more closely resemble living species with deeper strata containing species that were more dissimilar to living species, suggesting that life on Earth changed over time.
