## Possible Side effects (do they cause permanent harm)

- Has some chance of affecting [bone density](https://www.politifact.com/article/2023/aug/28/puberty-blockers-the-facts-and-the-myths/) as outlined by [this study](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/102/11/3904/4157556) however this can be easily reversed or countered with bone density medication.
- Puberty blockers are also reversible and have been used safely for decades to mitigate precocious puberty. 

## Age they are prescribed

Generally once the child starts puberty is the advised time, which can range from 8-14.

## Criteria of Administration

- Must meet the diagnostic criteria for [gender dysphoria](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gender-incongruence-and-transgender-health-in-the-icd) 

The [endocrine society](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/gender-dysphoria-gender-incongruence) has a similar criteria, mentioning:
> Diagnosing clinicians, mental health providers for adolescents, and mental health professionals for adults all should be knowledgeable about the diagnostic criteria for gender-affirming treatment, have sufficient training and experience in assessing related mental health conditions, and be willing to participate in the ongoing care throughout the endocrine transition.

### What is the endocrine transition 

This involves the use of hormones (e.g., estrogen or testosterone) to align an individual's physical characteristics with their gender identity. The term encompasses the initiation, monitoring, and ongoing management of hormone therapy, which is typically overseen by clinicians, including endocrinologists, in collaboration with mental health professionals and other healthcare providers.
## Non-Adequate Screening Claims

Some people have claimed online that puberty blockers are given out without adequate screening. An Endocrine Society spokesperson said the organization advises that the decision to start puberty blockers be "made only after extensive medical and mental health evaluations, extended discussions with the patient and their family, and a consideration of the potential risks and benefits of beginning or not beginning therapy."

## Regret Rate
A recent study (part of the _TransYouth Project_) surveyed ~220 youths who received puberty blockers or hormones. Mean follow-up ~4.9 years after starting blockers.
- Of those, 4 had stopped all gender-affirming medical care; 1 was continuing blockers but planned to stop. Others continued. [JAMA Network+1](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2825195)
- Dissatisfaction was similarly low as shown in [this article](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2825195).
- As a percentage the regret rate seems to be about *4%*. 
- For many people, benefits (psychiatric, emotional, quality of life) seem to outweigh these risks, particularly when treatment is well supervised, when support is strong, and when decisions are made carefully.
- Moreover the regret rate for **fully transitioning** is 1-2% and the vast majority report improved well-being and life satisfaction after transition. And its even rarer the people de-transition due to the realization that they're not trans.

#### Reason for regret
- It should be noted that the reason for regret isn't because they changed their mind because they're not trans anymore, or even the medication itself, rather because of the timing, the social consequences, the side effects, and so on). Also of note is that *stopping of treatment does not equal regret*.
- Regret due purely to identity misdiagnosis is rare.
