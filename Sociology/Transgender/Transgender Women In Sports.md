### Trans Women Performance in Sports

The argument is often made that TW[^4] have an innate biological advantage over biological women, with trans-women being biologically male. However, studies suggest[^2][^3] that this difference may not be as pronounced, if at all, with physiological differences decreasing over time. 

There are, however, edge cases. The IOC has noted that "With the rare exception of athletes with a diagnosis of complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS) or [other rare differences/disorders in sex development (DSDs)](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-07-11/caster-semenya-human-rights-south-african-athletics-women/102589402) who do not benefit from the anabolic and/or performance-enhancing effects of testosterone, no athlete with an SRY-positive screen is eligible for competition in the female category ⁠at an IOC event." The evidence, whilst at this point is quite limited due to this phenomenon being relatively new, does not currently point to TW having writ-large greater performance outcomes over cis-women.

TW also show a higher rate of injury likelihood as a result of GAHT (Gender affirming hormone therapy) than cis-women. According to a PubMed review of sports medicine considerations when caring for transgender athletes[^5], studies show that TW show risk for decreased bone mineral density and delayed physeal closure, increased risk of insufficiency fractures, increased risk of VTE, and a higher level of risk for ACL injury.

### T-Level Requirements

- According to [transresearch.org](https://www.transresearch.org.au/post/trans-women-in-sport) "\[transgender women must be\] Maintaining testosterone levels below a threshold (e.g. < 5 nmol/L[^1]) for a set duration (e.g. 24 months)." This can be evidenced in subsection 3.2.2 and 3.3.3 of the Eligibility Regulations for Transgender Athletes. ([[C3.5 Eligibility Regulations Transgender_.pdf|source]]) The International Olympic Committee uses the metric of 10 nmol/L testosterone as the sole factor that divides male and female divisions.


### Eligibility for Trans People to Participate in Sports

- Whilst each sporting body has their own regulations on the criteria for the eligibility for trans people in sports, many sport organizations enforce a fairly unilateral position.
