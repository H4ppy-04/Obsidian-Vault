God often treats other nations much more harshly than his own people, which undermines his fair, just and righteous nature.
## Idolatry Among Israel vs Other Nations

God killed off the Canaanites because of:
 - Idolatry
 - Child Sacrifice
 - Other biblical sins

But when Israel commits the same sins, they aren't punished as harshly, but instead they are exiled or get plagues.

## The "Golden Calf Incident"

The sins of the **Canaanites** worshiping false gods and doing child sacrifice seem to justify conquest (Deut. 18:9–12)
When **Israel** however adopts the *exact same practices* (e.g. under kings like Manasseh, 2 Kings 21) they are exiled with the promise of later restoration.

# Rebuttals

People will say:

> Well, God just favored Israel because without it, Jesus couldn't have been born, and the holy place where he will return couldn't have been established"

However, this entails a few things.

- **Undermines justice omnibenevolence challenge:**  
  “But that means God suspended justice just to get His plan to work. If He’s perfectly just, He shouldn’t need to play favorites to make things happen.”

- **Omnipotence challenge:**  
  “If God is all-powerful, couldn’t He have brought Jesus into the world without needing to protect Israel unfairly?”

- **Moral consistency:**  
  “So you’re saying other nations had to be wiped out so Israel could survive, but Israel could commit the same sins and still be spared. Doesn’t that mean God values His plan more than justice or fairness?”


*The Amalekites were genocide(d) based on what they did, not what they will do.*
