### Main argument

1. **P1.** Agents have interests in exercising their agency and pursuing their goals.
2. **P2.** A sufficient degree of freedom and basic wellbeing is necessary for agents to exercise their agency and pursue their goals.
3. **P3.** When an agent has a fundamental interest that is necessary for the exercise of its agency, that agent has a _prima facie_ moral claim against unjustified interference with that interest.
4. **P4.** These considerations apply to agents generally, not merely to me.
5. **C.** Therefore, agents generally have _prima facie_ rights against unjustified interference with the freedom and basic wellbeing necessary for the exercise of their agency.

### Supplementary argument for P3

1. **P1.** Agents have fundamental interests in exercising their agency and pursuing their goals.
2. **P2.** A sufficient degree of freedom and basic wellbeing is necessary for exercising agency.
3. **P3.** If an agent's fundamental interests have moral standing, those interests cannot be overridden merely because another agent prefers to interfere with them.
4. **P4.** Agents' fundamental interests have moral standing because they are interests belonging to beings capable of pursuing ends and being affected by interference with those ends.
5. **P5.** Without a moral protection against unjustified interference, an agent's ability to pursue its fundamental interests is left entirely at the discretion of other agents.
6. **P6.** It is unjustified to leave an agent's fundamental interests entirely at the arbitrary discretion of others when those interests are necessary for the exercise of its agency.
7. **C.** Therefore, agents have a _prima facie_ moral claim against unjustified interference with the freedom and basic wellbeing necessary for the exercise of their agency.
8. **C2.** Therefore, agents have _prima facie_ rights against such interference.


### Definitions

**Agent** — a being capable of having goals or ends and taking actions in pursuit of those goals.
**Agency** — the capacity of an agent to form, pursue, and act upon goals or ends.
**Interest** — a state of affairs that is beneficial to an agent's ability to pursue its goals or otherwise contributes to its flourishing.
**Fundamental interest** — an interest that is sufficiently basic to an agent's ability to function as an agent or pursue its goals that its substantial impairment significantly undermines that agency.
**Freedom** — the absence of sufficiently significant external constraints or coercion that prevent an agent from exercising its agency.
**Basic wellbeing** — the conditions of functioning and flourishing that are sufficiently necessary for an agent to exercise its agency, including things such as physical integrity, basic health, and avoidance of serious harm.
**Unjustified interference** — interference with an agent's interests that lacks sufficient moral justification, such as being done merely for another person's convenience, preference, or benefit when no stronger moral consideration outweighs the interference.
**Moral standing** — the status of having one's interests count as reasons that others are morally required to take into account.
**Moral claim** — a morally justified claim that others have a duty to refrain from certain actions or, where applicable, to perform certain actions toward the claimant.
**Right** — a morally justified claim that provides an agent with protection against specified forms of interference or harm by others.
**Prima facie right** — a right that ought to be respected unless it is overridden or defeated by a sufficiently strong competing moral consideration.
