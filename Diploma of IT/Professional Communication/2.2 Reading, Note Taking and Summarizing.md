4S system 

Searching: 

 - Use the key words in question to search for question: 

 - What is the topic of the text 

 - is it relevant to your question 

 - is the text reliable? 

 - Look at the table of contents or abstract if it's a journal. 

 - look at the headings or the figures 

 - find a variety of text types (books, articles, websites) 

 Skimming: 

- Read the text quickly on the surface:  
    
    - What is the general message? 
        
    - Does it link to the keywords in your prompt? 
        
    - Quickly look at the:  
        
        - Introduction 
            
        - Conclusion 
            
        - Topic sentences  
            
            - Typically the first sentence per paragraph. 
                
            
             Selecting: 
            
- Look for specific content to answer your research question 
    
- Be ruthless in selecting relevant content 
    
- Take careful note of what you have been asked to do. Whether that be to:  
    
    - discuss 
        
    - compare and contrast 
        
    - provide strategies or solutions 
        

Selecting: 

- Look for a variety of sources 
    

 Studying: 

- Carefully read your selected parts 
    
    - Understand the content 
        
    - Compare what different writers say about the topic 
        
    - Critically question what you read 
        
    - Research is very time consuming. 
        

Cornell System 

It's a system of note taking 

Highlighting, note taking and annotating is personalising the information. 

Annotating is like talking to the text, asking questions, etc. 

Colour coding highlights. 

Yellow: Something interesting 

Blue: Something interesting but more relevant to topic () 

Red: need to read more about (do I disagree? Do I need to read more? What do I need to read more about?) 

Pink: definitions 

Develop a grid with annotations. 

As you read you fill it out. Columns could be (authors, year, research questions, quotations, findings etc.) 

Most quotes should be indirect quotes. 

Types of academic papers: 

1. Empirical (always have research questions) 
    
2. Theoretical (developing a theory or comparing different theories) 
    
3. Conceptual (develops a new way of thinking about a topic) 
    
4. Systematic review (they review other papers, no experiment is conducted) 
    

Structure of a research paper 

- Abstract  
    
    - Summary of paper 
        
- Limitations 
    
    - Justifies any unexpected results 
        
- Introduction  
    
    - Research questions 
        
    - Overview of paper 
        
    - Justify why the research gap you are looking at is important 
        
    - Then formulate research questions based on the gap. 
        
- Method 
    
    - What did you do 
        
    - How did you do it 
        
    - Research instruments used (questionnaires, surveys, etc.) 
        
    
- Results 
    
    - Say whether findings are in line with previous studies. 
        
    - Present findings.  
        
    - Provided in multiple forms (text, tables, figures, etc.) 
        
- Discussion 
    
    - Why are the results the way they are? 
        
    - Are findings in line with consensus? 
        
- Future research: 
    
    - Say what else needs to be done or suggestions for future researchers. 
        
    - What is still missing from the research gap.
