Defining EQ 

 Emotional intelligence as defined by the source: 

Emotional intelligence, also known as EI or EQ, is the brain's ability to see feelings coming and then to use them, listen to what they've got to say, and manage them effectively. 

People that score highly in this metric can recognize and name emotions both internally and in others. Having this ability helps in different environments, especially in the workplace when dealing with frustrated or upset individuals. 

Models of EQ 

The most famous model of EQ was developed by psychologists John Mayer and Peter Salovey. They defined it within the scope of four primary skills: 

1. Perceiving - Our ability to detect emotions in faces, pictures, voices, and ourselves. (Axiomatic in EQ) 
    
2. Using - The ability to perceive changing moods and change what they do to channel their feelings effectively. 
    
3. Understanding - Our ability to comprehend the complex relationships between feelings, recognizing how moods evolve and change over time. 
    
4. Managing - The ability to regulate feelings in ourselves and influence those emotions in others.  
    

It should be noted that whilst some people are naturally gifted in this area, EQ can be improved through: 

- Mindfulness 
    
- Exercises 
    
- Therapy 
    
- Education 
    
- Healthy relationships 
    

Higher EQ correlates with higher academic success. People in good moods are better at inductive reasoning.

Emotional intelligence is made up of:  

-  Self-competence

        - Ability to self-assess their strengths and weaknesses and accept constructive feedback. 
            
    - Self-regulation  
        
        - Regulating or managing other states and impulses and resources 
            
        - Can keep disruptive emotions under control 
            
        - don't act impulsively 
            
        - usually trustworthy, ethical conscientious and organized

        - are innovative and consider alternative viewpoints

        - Strong achievement life 
            
        - results oriented

        - pursue goals beyond what is expected of them. 
            
        - Optimistic 
            
        - Persistent

    - Social-awareness  
        
        - Empathy 
            
        - Service 
            
        - Developing others

Benefits of emotional intelligence 

- Enhanced empathy 
    
- Improved conflict management skills 
    
- Better self-regulation 
    
- Helps building relationships 
    

Social awareness and relationship management 

- Awareness of others feelings, needs and concerns. 
    
- People with strong social awareness have:  
    
    - Service orientation  
        
        - Anticipating, recognizing and meeting others needs 
            
    - Develop other abilities  
        
        - Helping others to improve in order to develop and bolster their abilities. 
            
    - Ability to operate on constructive feedback 
        
    - make good mentors and coaches 
        
    - Understand the needs of other people 
        
- Social skills allow us to manage relationships effectively.  
    
    - People with good social skills:  
        
        - Good in leadership positions. 
            
        - Initiate change when necessary. 
            
        - Communicate openly  
            
            - Sends clear messages 
                
        - Influence others through persuasion to get support 
            
        - Respond with empathy to others 
            
        - Champion change 
            
        - Negotiate and manage conflict with difficult people 
            
        - Collaboration working with others towards shared goals 
            

Main takeaways 

- People that have high EQ are highly effective at identifying, managing their internal emotions and social environments.  
    
- Typically higher success rates socially and in business. 
    
- A higher EQ level can be very valuable traits in business and leadership positions.
