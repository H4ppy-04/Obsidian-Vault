- Feedback should highlight things that are both good and bad 
    
-  Feedback should be a cycle of refinement, and receiving feedback until a satisfactory result has been achieved.  
    
-  Feedback depends on the environment (work, academic, etc.) 
    
- Different types of feedback: 
    
    - Formal – in environments like professional 
        
    - Informal – in academic environments 
        
- Feedback should be constructive 
    
    - Aim to help the person improve 
        
        - "I noticed you were not in class the other day to help. Then say how the person can improve" 
            
    - Should avoid criticism. 
        
        - "You don't help with group work." 
            
- Feedback is based on past behavior 
    
    - Is uni-directional  
        
    - Given by one to one person 
        
- With coaching it's helping a person develop 
    
    - There's little evaluation as there's no past behavior 
        
    - Bi-directional 
        
- Feedback framework: 
    
    - S – Specific. 
        
    - M – Measurable. - Suggest specific behaviors, ideas, behaviors, or tasks. Generate ideas and plans which then in turn encourages reflection. 
        
    - A – Achievable. Do not overload your feedback, but rather focus on the most important issues. 
        
    - R – Relevant – Focus on actions. Tread cautiously with appearance, motives, etc. 
        
    - T – Timely – The closer to the event it is given the better. Temporal proximity is ideal. 
        

When giving feedback: 

-  Ask questions 
    
- Be specific 
    
- Explain why 
    
- be honest and respectful.
