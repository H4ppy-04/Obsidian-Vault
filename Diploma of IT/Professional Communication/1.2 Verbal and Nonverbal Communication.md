Verbal is just "using words" - can be both oral or written. 

- Helps us shape attitudes about the world. 
- Helps us think 
- Helps us organize complex ideas and experiences.  
- Helps us "define reality" 

 Non-verbal is communication without using words in any form. (gestures, facial expressions) 

 Syntax, semantics and context can influence language and meaning. If any of these are violated then communication breaks down. 

 Context can affect communication in the way that it can also be violated. (culture, formality) 

Improving verbal communication 

1. Define your terms. 
2. Choose precise words. (there might be better words to use) 
3. Consider your audience (relevant to first point) 
4. Control tone. What tone is best for the intended audience? (serious, friendly) 
5. Check for understanding 
6. Be results-oriented. (What do I intend to achieve at the end of the act of communication?) 

Formal and informal communication 

- Tone is more serious in formal vs casual 
- Vocabulary is more sophisticated in formal vs everyday language / slang / idioms. 
- In formal sentences follow strict sentence and grammar rules vs more relaxed and lacking punctuation  
- In formal, context is relevant. 

|   |   |
|---|---|
|Formal|Informal|
|I would like to request your assistance|Nice to meet you|
|Could you please provide me with the necessary information|Just letting you know about the meeting|
|I apologize for any inconvenienced caused|Sorry for the trouble|
|It is a pleasure to make your acquaintance|Can you help me out|
|I am writing to inform you about the upcoming meeting|Can you give me the info i need?|

Nonverbal cues can contradict verbal messages. 

Features of non-verbal communication: 

- Inevitable  
- A primary conveyor of emotion 
- Usually multi-channeled (combination of posture, gestures, body language, facial) 
- Can be ambiguous 

Functions of non-verbal communication: 
- To duplicate verbal communication (for example, say yes and nod head)  
    - It doesn't replace or add but it just duplicates what is said orally 
- Replaces verbal communication  
    - When oral communication is impossible you use non-verbal communication 
    - Hand wave or you gesture 
- Complement verbal communication  
    - Cannot be used alone without verbal message (Verbal messages may be ambiguous without non-verbal communication) 
    - For example, pointing to something and saying "give that to me" 
- Accent / emphasize verbal communication  
    - Putting stress on specific words ("do not do that") 
- Contradicting verbal and non-verbal communication  
    - We place greater value on non-verbal communication 
    - For example, someone asks "how are you" and depending on the tone, you can appear not fine, despite saying you are fine. 
- Transcend cultural norms (kisses, hugs)
