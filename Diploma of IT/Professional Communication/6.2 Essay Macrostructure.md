Structure of the essay. 

1. Introduction and Thesis statement – think of a funnel, start with general and the more you move down make it more focused until you get to the thesis statement (last sentence). Always keep your research question visible when writing your essay, preventing you from staying off topic. 
    
    1. To introduce the general topic 
        
    2. To provide background to the topic 
        
        1. The breadth of the topic (the ideas that you could but not necessarily discuss) 
            
    3. To indicate the importance of an issue 
        
    4. To identify something that we do not know 
        
    5. To state the position the author will argue 
        
    6. To provide an overview of what is to come 
        
    7. Define important terms if any 
        
    8. Provide a thesis statement (most important) 
        
2. Body paragraphs. 
    
3. Conclusion 
    
4. References 
    

Thesis statement 

1. Purpose: tell the readers what your position is about an issue or research question 
    
2. The thesis statement is the central focus of the essay. Your entire argument and supporting claims all focus on the thesis 
    
3. It will contain each of the key words you identify from your essay question, without repeating the question. 
    
4. Questions to ask before writing thesis: 
    
    1. What is my overall position? 
        
        1. Read on the literature before deciding 
            
    2. What do I want my readers to know after reading this essay? 
        
    3. Why would someone want to read this essay? (this one should come before thesis statement, after general statements) 
        

Structure of thesis statement (not necessarily in order): 

Context, stance, scope, conclusion (conclusion is optional) 

Context: Orient the reader and establish the foundation of the problem before introducing your main argument.  

Stance: Take a position that you will argue in your essay 

Scope: Define a domain in which your position applies 

Conclusion (Optional) 

Topic Sentences 

The topic sentence statement should tell the reader what the whole paragraph is about. 

It should be one of the 3 main ideas of the thesis statement. (don't include more than one, or one idea in a few paragraphs) 

It's used to signpost the rest of the paragraph. 

It's written in the writers voice. (No citations; it's your opinion). 

Short and sharp. 

Follows the same format for each body paragraph 

Provides a skeleton outline (collectively, when put together) 

There are 2 components of topic sentences (order not important) 

1. Controlling idea – the focus of the paragraph 
    
2. Main topic – the general topic you are writing about 
    

Main topic: the cause of pollution (essay question) 

Controlling idea: The more specific focus of the main topic 

Make sure your topic sentences are not too long.
