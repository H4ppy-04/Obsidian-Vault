Professional vs Non-professional: Professional is someone who has special training / qualifications or a degree. Often following a code of ethics or professional standards. A non-professional is someone who does not need special education or qualifications to do work; however, they still may need shorter term training.  

Introduction to Ethics in Professional Communication 

- Ethics: Moral principles that differentiate right from wrong, guiding what we "ought to" and "ought not to" do. 
    
- Ethical Communication: Ensures fairness, respect, and honesty in communication. 
    

Principles that guide communication practices that share: 

 - Fairness: Treating everyone equally . 

 - Respect: Valuing and considering the feelings of others. Demonstrating active listening. 

 - Honesty: Telling the truth. Be straightforward and sincere.

Johnson & Johnson case 

-  Set the precedent for handling extreme crisis events 
    
-  Withdrew all Tylenol even if this cost the company millions, resulting in public perception of the company being largely positive and Johnson & Johnson were viewed as the victims. 
    
- The company was proactive in media coverage ensuring that subsequent deaths were minimized. 
    
- Founded new pioneering technology as a result relatively quickly (tamper-resistant bottles) 
    
- "Johnson & Johnson provided the victim's families counseling and financial assistance even though they were not responsible for the product tampering" ([https://www.ou.edu/deptcomm/dodjcc/groups/02C2/Johnson%20&%20Johnson.htm](https://www.ou.edu/deptcomm/dodjcc/groups/02C2/Johnson%20&%20Johnson.htm)) 
    

Johnson & Johnson's failure to employ/establish a positive relationship with the media, a key stakeholder, forced the company to respond to the crisis in an advertising-like manner. Johnson & Johnson received criticism from the media for not being genuine due to the slick sales-like response ads run during the crisis. 

Why does leadership matter in ethical communication? 

 - Set the organizational tone and culture 

 - Direct impact on stakeholder relationships 

 - Policy and decision making 

Importance of Ethical Communication 

- Interpersonal Level: Builds credibility and trust. 
    
- Organizational Level: Promotes transparency, trust in leadership, and prevents legal issues. 
    
- Employee Satisfaction: Fosters a positive culture and encourages employee retention. 
    

Types of Ethics 

- Corporate Ethics: Values guiding organizational behavior. 
    
- Social Ethics: Principles governing interactions with society. 
    
- Cultural Ethics: Sensitivity to cultural differences. 
    
- Professional Ethics: Standards guiding professional conduct. 
    
- Personal Ethics: Individual values guiding behavior. 
    

Ethical Considerations in Communication 

- Transparency: Balancing openness with confidentiality. 
    
- Truthfulness and Honesty: Ensuring accurate information. 
    
- Respect and Empathy: Valuing all individuals and their perspectives. 
    
- Confidentiality: Protecting private information. 
    
- Cultural Sensitivity: Respecting cultural norms and differences. 
    
- Avoiding Plagiarism: Properly crediting sources. 
    
- Avoiding Bias and Discrimination: Treating everyone equitably. 
    
- Responsibility and Accountability: Owning one's actions and words. 
    

Role of Leadership in Ethical Communication 

- Policy and Decision-Making: Leaders set the organizational tone and culture. 
    
- Common Ethical Dilemmas: Balancing transparency, honest reporting, and ethical decision-making.
