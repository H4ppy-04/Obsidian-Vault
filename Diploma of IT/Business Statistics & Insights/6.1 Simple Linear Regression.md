### Class Notes

- In a positive linear relation, $\beta_1$ will be the slope of the regression line **(population slope)**, estimated change in $Y$ for every one unit change in $X$. 
	- $\beta_1$ will be $\gt 0$ in a positive linear relation
- $b_1$ is the **sample** of the slope, as opposed to $\beta_1$ which is the population slope.
	- Greek letters are used to represent population statistics
	- Roman letters are used to represent sample statistics
- 40% of the questions are around $b_1$ in #wsta1250/final 
	- Slope is just the rise/run
	- Intercept is a constant (doesn't change)
- If $\beta_1\gt 0$ then $b_1\gt 0$ because they hold a mutual relationship.
- If there is a scatter plot where the linear relationship is negative then $\beta_1\lt0$ and $b_1\lt0$. 
- If there is **no linear relation**, then $\beta_1=0$ and $b_1=0$. This is because rise is 0, so rise/run solve to 0.

### Hypothesis Testing for Linear Regression
==copy all this onto the cheat sheet== except for the $t$ test presumably.

To declare $H_0$ we may do:

$H_0:\beta_1=0$ (no significant linear relation)
$H_0:\beta_1\neq0$ (there exists a significant linear relation)
- If this is the case then either:
	1. $\beta_1\gt0$ so there is a positive relation
	2. $\beta_1\lt 0$ so there is a negative relation


>[!note]
>Always write the same hypothesis. You will never have to change the subscript according to the question. It will always be in this format.

#### Assumptions
1. Linearity of the relation
	1. Cases where there are parabolic or non-linear relations then there is no point conducting a hypothesis test. Also in cases where there is any curve in the line.
2. normality of the residuals.
	1. The histogram need to look like a normalized histogram (a bell curve)
3. constant standard deviation
	1. You will be shown a residual plot. this it to see if the residuals have a constant standard deviation. As shown in the figure, the dots need to be even around the horizontal line with no **pattern**. ==However, you won't be given either of these.==
		1. A **pattern** that is a violation is either (of 2)
			1. A **fanned out** pattern (horizontal parabolic) such that as $x$ increases the standard deviation increases
			2. A **fanned in** plot which is just the horizontal inverse of the previous pattern 1.

In this residual plot, the $x$ axis is in the center such that it is easy to see if there exists a constant standard deviation: (this is a valid pattern)

Regarding output table:

 - The others **will be given**.
 - In regression statistics table
	 - Observations is the sample size $n$.
	 - As always $df=n-2$.
 - In the last output table:
	 - The **intercept** is $b_0$
	 - The **age** is $b_1$ (coefficient of the intercept)
	 - ==You only need the first value from the **intercept** row.==
		 - The focus is always on the second line (x variable)
	 - The coefficient of the x varaible is the $b_1$ variable.
	 - The $t$ statistic can be found in the 3rd column of the last row.
	 - On intervals:
		 - The lower bound (lower 95%) is the penultimate column
		 - The upper bound (upper 95%) is the ultimate.
