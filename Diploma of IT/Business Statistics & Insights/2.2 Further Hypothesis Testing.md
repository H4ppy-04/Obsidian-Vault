### One-sample $t$-test.
#### Assumptions
- The data is sourced from a Normal distribution.[^1]
- ==Ensure that the $t$-distribution is robust against non-normality for large samples.==

#### Requirements
 - Sample standard distribution
 - Sample size
 - Sample mean
 - Estimated or 'claimed' population mean $\mu_0$ 
#### Formula

$$t=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}$$


### Two-sample $t$-test

#### What this is

>[!todo]
>explain what this is used for

#### Assumptions
 - Both samples are sourced from Normal distributions
 - Both samples are drawn from populations with equal spread. ($\sigma_1=\sigma_2$)

#### Requirements
 - Population standard distribution for both $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$.
 - Sample mean for both samples 
 - $n$ values for both samples

#### Defining $H_0$

>[!important]
>In the final exam, none of the normal assumptions will be violated.

In the final exam there will be a comparative box plot to analyze the difference in spread. For this question, use the IQR. Whenever comparing the spread we are only looking the IQR.


>[!note]
>$n_1$ and $n_2$ don't have to be the same.

#### Formula

$$t=\frac{\bar{y}_1-\bar{y}_2}{S_p\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1}+\frac{1}{n_2}}}$$
With: $$df=n_1+n_2-2$$
#### Calculating Pooled Standard Deviation

 - This is the weighted average of the two-sample standard deviation
 - This is used because we are assuming that both samples are drawn from populations with the same standard deviation. (which is why earlier we assumed $\sigma_1=\sigma_2$)

>[!tip]
>A **Rule of Thumb** for what is acceptable tolerance for the assumption $\sigma_1=\sigma_2$ is to see if the ratio falls from 0.5 to 2. That is, neither sample standard deviation is more than twice
the other. (This is often inferred by comparing the IQR’s of a comparative box plot).

- So, we calculate $S_p$ to estimate $\sigma_P$, where $\sigma_P$ is the common population standard deviation. $$S_p=\sqrt{\dfrac{s^2_1(n_1-1)+s^2_2(n_2-1)}{n_1+n_2-2}}$$

### Anaconda Length Scenario

$H_0:\mu=3$
$H_0:\mu\ne 3$ 

Assumptions:
 - The question does not tell us if the distribution is normal
 - However, the question does give us a graph that shows us that there is a normalized histogram. Therefore we conclude that this is a data set that we can work with.

Given

$$t=\frac{\bar{y}-\mu}{\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}}$$

2.75/(11.76/7)
>[!note]
>If you see the letter `E` then that means 10 zeroes. For instance $E^{-7}$ means $\times 10^{-7}$ If you see this, just write $p\lt 0.05$
>

In this case $p\lt 0.05$ so reject $H_0$.

Solution: P=`3.32211E-06`

In this scenario we reject the null hypothesis that the average length of anacondas is equal to 3 meters because the p value is less than the significance value of 0.05
