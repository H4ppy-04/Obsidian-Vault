 - First population enumeration of the establishment of Israel conducted by the central bureau of statistics
 - Conducted November 8, 1948
 - Objective: To establish an official Population Register and issue identity certificates for residents ahead of the first Knesset (parliament) elections.

The argument is that since Palestinians were displaced and the order specified anyone not in their home wouldn't be counted so it disproportionately affected Arabs. 


Pro Israel argument:
 - Many countries conducting wartime censuses or registrations count only those physically present.
 - The argument is that the registration rule applied to everyone, regardless of whether they were Jewish or Arab.
 - The government feared infiltration by armed combatants and the return of people who might assist enemy forces.
- Restricting registration to those physically present made it easier to distinguish residents from people crossing borders after hostilities.
- Roughly 150,000 Palestinian Arabs remained within Israel after the war.
- Most eventually became Israeli citizens (although many lived under military administration until 1966).
- Pro-Israel advocates point to this as evidence that Arab ethnicity alone did not bar citizenship.
