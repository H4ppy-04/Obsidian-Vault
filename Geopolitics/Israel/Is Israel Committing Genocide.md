1. According to the CPPCG (Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the crime of Genocide) Article 2 https://treaties.un.org/doc/publication/unts/volume%2078/volume-78-i-1021-english.pdf
	1. "The intent is the most difficult element to determine. To constitute genocide, there must be a proven intent on the part of perpetrators to physically destroy a national, ethnic, racial or religious group. Cultural destruction *does not suffice*, not does an intention to simply disperse a group. It is this special intent, or ***dolus specialis***, that makes the crime of genocide so unique. In addition, case law has associated intent with the existence of a State or organizational plan or policy, even if the definition of genocide in international law does not include that element."
	2. This is different from *mens rea* (Criminal intent, Latin for "the guilty mind") as a categorically higher standard to prove. This is all outlined in the ICC case.
2. Dolus Specialis differs from just **dolus eventualis** (recklessness)
