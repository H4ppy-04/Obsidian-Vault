# Constitutional Evidence: Secularism is Explicit

## The U.S. Constitution (1787)

The U.S. Constitution is the supreme law of the land, and it never mentions Christianity, Jesus, or the Bible.  
The only references to religion are explicitly secular:

- **Article VI, Clause 3:**
    
    > “No religious Test shall ever be required as a Qualification to any Office or public Trust under the United States.”  
    > → This was groundbreaking at the time, as most European nations required religious affiliation for public office.
    
- **First Amendment (1791, Bill of Rights):**
    
    > “Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof.”  
    > → Establishes separation of church and state by preventing the government from endorsing or imposing any religion.

## II. The Treaty of Tripoli (1797) – A Direct Statement Against Christian Foundations

- **Signed by President John Adams, Ratified Unanimously by the Senate:**
    
    > “The government of the United States of America is not, in any sense, founded on the Christian religion.”  
    > → No ambiguity. This was a formal treaty, not private correspondence, making it one of the clearest official statements on the matter.  
    > The U.S. Senate ratified it unanimously. There was no controversy, showing that this view was unproblematic to the Founders.  
    > If the U.S. were truly a Christian Nation, such a statement would have been impossible to pass without public outrage.

## III. Quotes from the Founding Fathers: Anti-Christianity, Deism, and Secularism

### 1. Thomas Jefferson (Principal Author of the Declaration of Independence)

- **Against Christianity’s Role in Government:**
    
    > “Christianity neither is, nor ever was a part of the common law.”  
    > —Letter to Dr. Thomas Cooper, 1814
    
- **Support for Separation of Church & State:**
    
    > “I contemplate with sovereign reverence that act of the whole American people which declared that their legislature should ‘make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof,’ thus building a wall of separation between Church & State.”  
    > —Letter to the Danbury Baptists, 1802
    
- **Criticism of the Bible’s Influence:**
    
    > “The Christian God is a being of terrific character—cruel, vindictive, capricious, and unjust.”  
    > —Jefferson’s Personal Bible Notes
    
- **Jefferson’s Deist Beliefs:**
    
    > “It does me no injury for my neighbor to say there are twenty gods or no god. It neither picks my pocket nor breaks my leg.”  
    > —_Notes on Virginia_, 1782
    
- **Jefferson’s “Bible” (The Jefferson Bible, 1820):**  
    → Jefferson literally cut out all supernatural and divine elements from the New Testament, keeping only the moral teachings of Jesus.
    

---

### 2. James Madison (“Father of the Constitution”)

- **Christianity is dangerous when mixed with government:**
    
    > “What influence, in fact, have ecclesiastical establishments had on society? In some instances, they have been seen to erect a spiritual tyranny on the ruins of the civil authority; in many instances, they have been seen upholding the thrones of political tyranny; in no instance have they been the guardians of the liberties of the people.”  
    > —_Memorial and Remonstrance Against Religious Assessments_, 1785
    
- **Against Religious Influence in Law:**
    
    > “The civil government functions with complete success by the total separation of the church from the state.”  
    > —Letter to Edward Livingston, 1822
    
- **On Religious Freedom in Government:**
    
    > “Religion & government will both exist in greater purity, the less they are mixed together.”  
    > —Letter to Edward Livingston, 1822
    

---

### 3. John Adams (Signer of the Treaty of Tripoli, 2nd President)

- **Explicit Rejection of America as a Christian Nation:**
    
    > “The government of the United States is not, in any sense, founded on the Christian religion.”  
    > —_Treaty of Tripoli_, 1797
    
- **Skepticism of Religion’s Role in Society:**
    
    > “This would be the best of all possible worlds if there were no religion in it.”  
    > —Letter to Thomas Jefferson, 1817
    
- **On the Danger of Religious Power:**
    
    > “The divinity of Jesus is made a convenient cover for absurdity.”  
    > —Letter to Jefferson, 1813
    

---

### 4. Benjamin Franklin (Deist, Scientific Thinker)

- **Against Religious Superstition:**
    
    > “Lighthouses are more useful than churches.”  
    > —_Poor Richard’s Almanack_, 1758
    
- **Skepticism of Jesus’ Divinity:**
    
    > “As to Jesus of Nazareth, my opinion of whom you particularly desire, I think his system of morals and his religion, as he left them to us, the best the world ever saw or is likely to see; but I apprehend it has received various corrupting changes, and I have… some doubts as to his divinity.”  
    > —Letter to Ezra Stiles, 1790
    

---

### 5. Thomas Paine (Author of _Common Sense_ & _The Age of Reason_)

- **Anti-Christianity, Pro-Deism:**
    
    > “I do not believe in the creed professed by the Jewish church, by the Roman church, by the Greek church, by the Protestant church, nor by any church that I know of. My own mind is my own church.”  
    > —_The Age of Reason_, 1794

- **Christianity is Mythology:**
    > “The Christian religion is a parody on the worship of the Sun.”  
    > —_The Age of Reason_
- **The Bible is Fraudulent:**
    > “Of all the tyrannies that affect mankind, tyranny in religion is the worst.”

### 6. George Washington (More Neutral, But Avoided Christianity in Politics)

- Refused to take communion as President.
- Deliberately avoided public religious rituals.
- Avoided Christian rhetoric in his _Farewell Address_ (1796).
- Unlike European monarchs who ruled “by divine right,” Washington’s farewell made no mention of Jesus or Christianity.

## V. Conclusion: The Founding Was Clearly Secular

- The Constitution deliberately avoids Christianity.
- Key Founders explicitly opposed religious influence.
- The Treaty of Tripoli outright denies Christian foundations.
- Many Founders were Deists or skeptics, not orthodox Christians.
